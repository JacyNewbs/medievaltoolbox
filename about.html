About

Free research tools for medieval English history

What this is

Medieval Toolbox is a small collection of free tools for students and scholars working with the records of medieval England. It grew out of the everyday problems of archival work: turning a regnal year into a date, finding which saint’s day a document was dated by, adding up accounts in pounds, shillings and pence, and tracking down where a class of Crown records has been printed and where the originals can be seen.

It is an independent, not-for-profit project. There are no adverts, no accounts to create, and nothing to pay.

The tools

  • Regnal Year Converter Anno Regni
    Converts regnal years to modern dates and back, from William I to Charles III, down to the day and month.
  • Medieval Calendar Calendarium
    Saints’ days, moveable feasts, law terms and return days for any year.
  • Money & Units Nummus
    A pounds, shillings and pence calculator, with converters for coin denominations and medieval weights and measures.
  • Sources Fontes
    A guide to the records of the medieval English Crown to 1509, from Chancery, the Exchequer, the courts and the Special Collections, with links to free scans of the printed calendars, images of the original rolls, and searches of The National Archives’ catalogue.

More tools are in development and will be added here as they are ready.

Who made it

Medieval Toolbox is built and maintained by Jacob Newbury (LinkedIn), a PhD researcher at the University of Winchester studying a gentry family in fifteenth-century Dorset, and documentary researcher.

Using what you find here

The toolbox is a finding aid. Dates, values and references should be checked against the original documents and the printed editions they come from, and it is those you should cite in your own work. The Sources pages give the full details of every edition they link to.

The tools draw on standard reference works, chiefly C.R. Cheney, A Handbook of Dates for Students of British History, revised by Michael Jones (Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2000), and R.E. Zupko, British Weights and Measures: A History from Antiquity to the Seventeenth Century (Madison: University of Wisconsin Press, 1977).

Privacy

Medieval Toolbox does not use cookies and does not ask for any personal information.

Visits are counted with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which records anonymous totals (pages viewed, the site you came from, country, and browser and device type) without cookies and without identifying or following you across other sites. The Sources page remembers which tab you last had open; this is kept in your own browser and is never sent anywhere.

Links to other websites, such as The National Archives or the Internet Archive, take you to sites with their own privacy policies.
